## Documented Product Reasoning (Not a Scope or Constitutional Change)

Recorded per Jamie & Solara so this reasoning doesn't depend on thread memory. Nothing here changes MVP scope (BYG-PRD-001) or the Constitution (BYG-GOV-001) — it strengthens the *rationale* behind decisions already locked (#6, #11, #13).

**The Preparation → Confidence → Courage mechanism.** GOV-001 §6 states courage is one of the products BYG delivers, and that the learner already possesses it. The elaborated mechanism: *preparation reduces uncertainty → reduced uncertainty increases confidence → confidence makes courage easier to use.* This doesn't change §6 — it explains *how* §6 works, and should inform product decisions (e.g., does a proposed feature actually reduce uncertainty, or just add content?).

**"A personal library of courage" — internal language only.** This phrase describes what the My Ready Packs library (Decision #13) represents: each saved Ready Pack is a conversation the learner chose to prepare for instead of avoid. **This is explicitly not proposed public marketing copy** — it's internal product language for how the team should think about what the library means, distinct from anything shown to a learner. Keep this distinction if the phrase is ever considered for outward-facing use; that would be a separate, deliberate decision, not an automatic promotion from internal reasoning to copy.

**Strategic rationale for Saved Ready Packs, beyond the mechanics.** The value of the library isn't that AI responses persist — it's that the learner returns to preparation they've already made instead of starting from a blank prompt every time, and the library becomes more valuable the longer someone owns it (without becoming relational — the practical/relational memory boundary, GOV-001 §8, is unchanged). This is one of the strongest answers found so far to "why not just use ChatGPT" (Decision #11): raw ChatGPT produces conversations; BYG accumulates an organized library of the learner's real life. Illustrative (non-binding) collection examples surfaced in discussion: Spain Trip, a pet's name, a partner's name, Work, Family — real evidence for why Collections (Decision #16) matters as a fast-follow, not a reason to pull it into V1.

**The "understanding loop" (three-part personalization principle).** Feeling understood is treated as happening in three stages: before generation (the app advances the learner's stated situation into a clear preparation plan, per the Understanding Checkpoint Rule, BYG-PRD-001 §17), inside the Ready Pack (content mirrors the full real-life situation rather than flattening it into one obvious conversation), and after the Ready Pack (Decision #17 — a small number of genuinely related next situations). Working product principle: *"A Ready Pack should make the learner feel understood before it makes them feel prepared — and understood again when it helps them see what may come next."* **This is recorded as product reasoning, not elevated to BYG-GOV-001.** Neither Jamie nor Solara asked for a Constitution amendment this round, and per the revised Amendment Principle (BYG-GOV-001 §13, Decision #15) that's a deliberate, explicit decision to make — not an automatic promotion. If this principle proves as foundational as the Preparation → Confidence → Courage mechanism above, that's a future call for Jamie and Solara to make explicitly.
