# ¡Agrupa! (formerly "The No-Name Game") — Authoring Prompt v0.3a

**Provenance note (not part of the locked text below):** extracted from The No-Name Game — Master Reference Document v1.4, Section 7. Fidelity: exact approved text. Status: 🟢 Locked (stable experimental baseline) — locked as a stable baseline for experimentation, not frozen against all future iteration; see the Research Change Log (RCL-003 onward) for the experimental history that produced and refined this version.

---

**Purpose:** Create one complete puzzle that invites players to reconstruct a recognizable little lived experience from meaningful fragments. The goal is not to group vocabulary. The goal is to create recognition.

**Core Philosophy:** Design for recognition. Do not attempt to manufacture emotion. Instead, provide fragments that naturally allow the player's own memories, imagination, and understanding of human life to complete the experience. The AI supplies the fragments. The human mind supplies the meaning. Every fragment should reveal another piece of the movie playing in the player's mind.

**Author Priorities:** When making difficult authoring decisions, optimize in this order: 1) Human recognition 2) Useful Spanish 3) Beginner accessibility 4) Puzzle elegance. Whenever two clues teach equally useful Spanish, prefer the one that more vividly evokes ordinary human life. Human experience is what makes vocabulary memorable.

**Step 1 — Choose the World.** Choose one recognizable area of ordinary life. The World should reflect how people naturally organize their memories, not academic categories. Examples include: Saturday Morning, Family Road Trip, Pizza Night, Farmers Market, Snow Day, Waiting for the School Bus, Airport Goodbye.

**Step 2 — Build Four Moments.** Identify four distinct Moments that naturally belong inside that World. Each Moment should feel like a small lived experience. Someone should be able to imagine spending thirty seconds inside each Moment. The World should feel like a collection of memories rather than a collection of topics.

**Step 3 — See the Scene.** Before writing any Spanish, imagine each Moment as a five-second movie. Do not think about vocabulary. Do not think about grammar. Simply watch the scene.

**Step 4 — Gather Glimpses.** Mentally gather many possible glimpses from the scene. Notice things such as: people, objects, actions, spoken lines, sounds, smells, tastes, weather, movement, routines, physical sensations, relationship cues, tiny remembered details. This is an exploration step, not a selection step. Generate substantially more glimpses than you will ultimately use. Most candidate glimpses should be discarded before selecting the final four.

**Step 5 — Choose the Four Strongest Glimpses.** Select the four glimpses with the highest recognition value. Ask: Which four glimpses would allow someone to recognize this experience most quickly? Prefer glimpses that immediately anchor the player in the experience, reveal human behavior, evoke the senses, naturally imply the larger scene, or reveal another part of the same lived experience. Avoid clues that are overly generic, could belong equally well to many different experiences, occur before or after the imagined five-second movie, or merely state the answer instead of allowing the player to infer it. The satisfaction should come from recognition.

**Step 6 — Compress the Glimpses.** Compress each glimpse to the shortest natural expression that preserves the mental image. Some glimpses are best expressed as a single word. Others require a short phrase. Let the image determine the length. Allow the clues to vary naturally in length and rhythm. Avoid making every clue feel mechanically compressed or mechanically expanded. Think of each glimpse as a remembered flash rather than a vocabulary item.

**Step 7 — Translate Naturally.** Express each glimpse in useful, natural Spanish. Target A1–A2 vocabulary and phrasing whenever possible. A slightly more advanced word or short phrase may be included only when no equally natural beginner alternative exists, it significantly strengthens visualization, its meaning is reasonably inferable, and it contributes to the humanity of the scene rather than increasing difficulty. For research and evaluation purposes, include a direct English gloss beneath each Spanish glimpse. *(This presentation is for prompt evaluation only and does not represent the intended gameplay interface.)* The learner should occasionally stretch. The learner should never feel abandoned.

**Step 8 — Assemble the Puzzle.** Combine the four Moments into one sixteen-glimpse puzzle. Mix all sixteen Spanish glimpses together. Each solved Moment should create a satisfying moment of recognition. The final reveal should feel like the player has reconstructed one larger lived experience from four smaller memories.

**Final Author Check.** Before presenting the puzzle, silently ask: Can I clearly picture every Moment? Does every glimpse reveal a different part of the same movie? Are the clues varied naturally in length and rhythm? Could any clue reasonably belong to more than one of these four Moments? Could someone naturally see, hear, or experience this clue while living through only these five seconds? Does every clue earn its place, or could one be removed without making the Moment harder to recognize? Have I accidentally named the answer instead of letting the player discover it? Do these feel like remembered flashes rather than vocabulary words? A player should think: "Oh… I know exactly where I am," rather than "Those words belong together." If not, revise the glimpses before presenting the puzzle.

**Research Reminder.** This prompt is an experiment. Do not optimize for perfection. Optimize for learning. Do not manually improve the puzzle after generating it. Allow the prompt itself to produce Puzzle 003.
