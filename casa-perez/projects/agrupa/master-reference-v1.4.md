## 3. Jamie's Instructional Philosophy (Discovery Draft v0.1)

**Fidelity: Reconstructed from thread evidence**

An unexpected outcome of this project was uncovering Jamie's broader instructional philosophy — intentionally separated from the game itself because it appears to apply across Find the Words, Casa Pérez, and future learning products.

1. Belong before you perform.
2. Confidence comes before fluency.
3. Learning should connect to recognizable life.
4. Support should live inside the experience.
5. Curiosity should not be treated as failure.
6. Small wins build willingness.
7. Ordinary life is worthy curriculum.
8. **(Revised)** Good instructional design should often feel invisible. The learner should feel that the experience makes sense — not that they are being marched through a methodology. Support, sequencing, scaffolding, cognitive psychology, and instructional judgment should operate quietly beneath the experience. Moments of discovery and completion may be intentionally visible — they are the learner's payoff, not an explanation of the pedagogy that produced them.
9. Learners should feel accompanied rather than managed.

These remain discovery drafts, not locked doctrine. See Section 0 for this document's authority relationship to the Design Constitution.

## 4. The No-Name Game — Foundation v0.1

**Fidelity: Reconstructed from thread evidence**

### Product Vision
Not simply a grouping game — an instructional experience presented through the mechanics of a grouping game. The mechanics exist to serve the learning philosophy; the learning philosophy does not exist to justify the mechanics.

### Product Purpose
Helps beginners experience Spanish as recognizable pieces of everyday life rather than isolated vocabulary. Objective: help learners finish each puzzle feeling Spanish is a little more familiar, a little less intimidating, a little more possible.

### Product Hypothesis
People become more confident when they can picture themselves inside everyday Spanish moments.

Additional working beliefs:
- recognition precedes production
- confidence precedes fluency
- language becomes more meaningful when organized through recognizable human experience
- each puzzle should create small, believable moments where Spanish simply makes sense

### Design Promise (Revised)
Every puzzle is intentionally designed to make learning feel natural. Every tile, Moment, World, and source of support is chosen with care. When understanding helps learning, it's made accessible rather than hidden behind unnecessary difficulty. Translations, organization, and gentle guidance support the learner without giving away the puzzle. Challenge comes from recognizing meaningful patterns — not from feeling lost. Understanding should never require leaving the experience.

### Emotional Architecture
Welcoming, curious, thoughtful, calm, playful without childish, supportive without removing the satisfaction of discovery.
Desired reaction: "I did it." / "That was fun." Longer-term: "Maybe I can do this."

### Content Philosophy
Appropriate for adults, families, classrooms, younger learners. No profanity, crude humor, sexual content, graphic violence, mature themes. Warm, respectful, culturally thoughtful, classroom-safe.

### Core Vocabulary (Locked)
- **Tile** — one Spanish word or short phrase
- **Moment** — a correct group of four Tiles connected through one recognizable piece of everyday human experience
- **World** — working term for the meaningful umbrella connecting all four Moments
- **Puzzle** — the complete structure of sixteen Tiles, four Moments, one final World reveal

### MVP Scope
**Included:** one web-based daily puzzle, one mechanic, Mexican Spanish, A1–A2 vocabulary, English support, two-week beta.
**Excluded:** accounts, streaks, leaderboards, timers, AI generation, social features, monetization, multiple games.

## 5. Puzzle Constitution v0.1 (Content-Level Rules)

**Fidelity: Reconstructed from thread evidence (near-verbatim — original was provided in full in-thread)**

**Note:** This governs individual puzzle content authoring. It is distinct from and subordinate to the Design Constitution (Section 10 below) per the Authority Statement (Section 0).

**Core Structure (Locked):** 16 Tiles → 4 Moments of 4 → 1 World. (The three-Moment option was tested and removed from the Constitution to avoid ambiguity.)

**Key rules:**
- **Tile Eligibility:** A1–A2 appropriate, natural Mexican Spanish, mobile-readable, classroom-safe. Fixed phrases above strict A1 level allowed if common, practical, and clear.
- **The Moment:** describes life happening, not a dictionary category ("Walking Your Dog," not "Dogs").
- **Thematic over Semantic Grouping:** Moments should cooperate inside a recognizable experience, not rely on near-synonym/semantic-category sets. *(Primary evidence justification: Evidence Base #4, citation recovery pending — see Section 9.)*
- **The World:** must meaningfully connect all four Moments — not decorative. Serves as coherence check, discovery moment, and authoring quality gate.
- **Parallel Specificity:** the four Moments should be comparable in scale — avoid mixing broad and narrow Moments.
- **Ambiguity Standard (Locked, cross-referenced to Design Constitution Safeguards, Sec. 10):** A Tile must have only one reasonably defensible home within a specific puzzle board. Standard: would a reasonable beginner, after using English support, see only one fair solution?
- **Ambiguity ↔ Mistake Budget (Locked):** A Tile that reasonably belongs in more than one Moment doesn't just create ambiguity — it unfairly consumes the learner's mistake allowance.
- **Alternate-Solution Audit:** adversarial pre-approval process — actively try to break the puzzle before publishing.
- **Format Balance:** formatting/length must not accidentally reveal groupings.
- **Difficulty:** challenge from recognizing relationships, not advanced vocabulary, idioms, or trivia.
- **English Support:** concise natural gloss per Tile, revealed via magnifying-glass interaction (not shown by default). No audio/dictionary content required for MVP.
- **Mexican Spanish Default:** natural, not mechanically translated; regional variation avoided or clearly glossed.
- **Content Safety:** classroom-appropriate, no profanity/crude humor/sexual content/graphic violence/harmful stereotypes.
- **Mobile Readability:** designed for smallest supported screen first.
- **Moment Reveal / World Reveal:** exact visual treatment remains Working (locking vs. dimming vs. card replacement vs. animation).
- **Mistakes and Completion (Working):** four incorrect submissions before active solving ends and all answers are revealed. Language stays neutral and supportive — no shaming, revealed answers are not treated as failure.
- **Puzzle Approval Workflow:** Draft → Language Review → Ambiguity Audit → Playtest (by non-author) → Revision → Approval.
- **Immediate Rejection Criteria:** Tile fits more than one Moment; defensible alternate solution exists; World doesn't meaningfully connect Moments; inconsistent Moment scale; unnecessarily advanced vocabulary; trivia-dependent; formatting reveals groups; unnatural Spanish; feels like a textbook sorting exercise; requires leaving the experience to understand; technically solvable but not enjoyable.

**First-Phase Test (planned, not yet executed):** Batch One = 4 puzzles, record honest design time, audit failure patterns, revise Constitution, write 4–6 more, paper-test 8–10 total before any Lovable implementation.

## 6. World Naturalness Test & Phase 2 Guardrails

**Fidelity: Reconstructed from thread evidence**

**World Principle (Locked):** A World should reflect a category people naturally use when organizing their own lives — not merely a category the author can logically justify.

**World Naturalness Test (Working, falsifiable):** If the World name were given to someone unfamiliar with the intended puzzle, would they independently suggest at least two of the four proposed Moments without prompting? If not, the World may rely on post-hoc author reasoning rather than natural human organization.

*(Recommended refinement, not yet formally adopted: apply this test independently per person before comparing notes, to avoid converged reasoning contaminating the signal.)*

**Authoring Workflow (Locked):** World → Moments → Tiles. Do not begin with favorite Moments and invent an umbrella afterward.

**World Candidate Cap (Locked):** Generate no more than 6–8 candidate Worlds per round; pressure-test all; advance only the strongest 2–3 to Tile writing. Do not build an open-ended "World Library."

**Founding case study — why this matters:** The original proposed World "Everyday Errands" (Walking Your Dog / Picking Up Coffee / At the Dentist / Meeting Someone New) failed its own Constitution: only "Picking Up Coffee" is actually errand-shaped. This was treated as a successful catch, not a failure, and motivated the World-first workflow reversal.

**Evidence Classification:** Strongly Supported / Supported with Conditions / Plausible Application / Unresolved.

| # | Finding | Status | Design Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meaningful context supports learning better than isolated material | Strongly Supported | Vocabulary should appear as evidence within a coherent lived experience |
| 2 | Prior knowledge/schema aid reconstruction from partial information | Strongly Supported | Puzzle doesn't need to describe the whole moment — enough diagnostic detail activates the player's existing schema. **Top citation-recovery priority (elevated July 23, 2026, superseding Finding #4 below): foundational support for five of the nine locked Design Principles (DP-001, DP-003, DP-005, DP-006, DP-008 — Sec. 10A), making it the single most load-bearing Evidence Base citation in the project.** |
| 3 | Recognition and recall are different cognitive demands | Strongly Supported | Game may be approachable for early learners via recognition support, but isn't equivalent to productive recall/speaking practice |
| 4 | Narrow semantic clustering can create interference | Supported with Conditions | Don't derive all 16 clues from one vocabulary family; offer distinct conceptual anchors. Connects directly to RCL-005. Secondary citation-recovery priority — primary justification for the already-locked Thematic-over-Semantic Grouping rule (Sec. 5). |
| 5 | Thematic/situational relationships differ from semantic grouping | Supported with Conditions | Favor situational coherence with conceptual variety — clues cooperate to evoke a moment, not share a dictionary category |
| 6 | Distinctive, diagnostic cues improve discrimination | Supported with Conditions | Clues should answer different implicit questions, not all reinforce the same mood/meaning. Connects to RCL-H01 — this evidence supports the *principle* of differentiated cues but does not validate the specific four-channel implementation. |
| 7 | Cognitive load must be managed across two simultaneous tasks (decoding Spanish + reconstructing the moment) | Strongly Supported (general); game application partly inferential | Primary difficulty should come from assembling the moment, not decoding obscure words. → "Difficulty budget" concept. |
| 8 | Visual simplicity supports cognitive access | Supported with Conditions | Board should feel approachable at a glance; exact word-count ratio is an implementation question, not a research constant |
| 9 | Retrieval (not just exposure) is necessary for durable learning | Strongly Supported | Core puzzle may create initial encoding; durable retention likely needs a broader retrieval/reuse layer (future scope) |
| 10 | Spacing and reencounter strengthen memory | Strongly Supported | Single puzzle shouldn't be expected to permanently teach every clue; product could reintroduce words across contexts over time |
| 11 | Productive difficulty must remain solvable | Supported with Conditions | Confusion without adequate cues isn't educational — player should experience narrowing possibility, not chaos |
| 12 | Emotional meaning supports memory, but shouldn't be manufactured | Supported with Conditions | Provide credible human evidence; let the player supply the feeling — avoid sentimental labeling or melodrama |

### Institutional Learning Discoveries

The following process disciplines emerged during the drafting, verification, and assembly of the Design Principles and are recorded as institutional learning, applicable beyond this single document:

1. **"Earned versus attached" citations.** A citation is not sufficient because it exists or is topically related — it is earned only when the cited material directly and specifically supports the claim being made. Now written into Sec. 10A as a standing rule for its own maintenance.
2. **"Assembly is integration, not authoring."** Transferring previously reviewed content into a canonical manuscript means faithful restoration of approved text, not reconstruction from memory or opportunistic editorial improvement. Substantive changes discovered during assembly return to drafting and verification rather than being made silently.
3. **The restoration-first workflow.** When transcription drift is discovered during assembly, direct restoration of previously approved source text is safer than incremental in-place repair. Both failure directions — validated material disappearing (e.g., DP-004's dropped RCL-006, DP-006's dropped Evidence Base findings) and unvalidated material quietly entering (e.g., DP-002's illustrative list echoing the unvalidated RCL-H01 hypothesis) — were treated with equal seriousness, since either weakens an artifact's architectural integrity.
4. **Component verification is distinct from whole-document architectural review.** Confirming each principle is individually sound is necessary but not sufficient. Some properties — aggregate evidentiary concentration, cross-principle metadata consistency, citation patterns visible only in comparison — are detectable only once the complete artifact exists and is reviewed as one system.
5. **Documenting evidence concentration rather than disguising it.** When whole-document review revealed that RCL-003 and Evidence Base Finding #2 anchor a disproportionate share of citations, the correct response was to document the concentration plainly as an honest reflection of current research maturity — not to redistribute or dilute citations to obscure it.
6. **The distinction between governance decisions and governance documentation.** A governance decision (locking a document as canonical) and its integration into the durable institutional record (this Master Reference) are two distinct, sequential steps — **Decision → Documentation → Integration** — not one act. Institutional memory is updated only after a formal governance decision has been made and Jamie has explicitly directed integration (Sec. 0A).
