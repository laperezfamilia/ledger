### Relationship to the Instructional Philosophy

This Constitution specializes Jamie's broader Instructional Philosophy for the specific design of The No-Name Game.

Where no conflict exists, the broader Instructional Philosophy remains the governing educational philosophy. This Constitution defines how those educational values are expressed within this particular game.

### Purpose

The No-Name Game exists to help players reconstruct recognizable human experiences through meaningful fragments of language.

Recognition—not categorization—is the organizing principle of the game.

Human experience is the subject.

Language is the medium.

The designer supplies evidence.

The player supplies recognition.

The puzzle is complete only when the player's own understanding reconstructs the hidden moment.

### Constitutional Principles

**Principle 1 — Recognition Over Categorization**

The purpose of the puzzle is to reconstruct a recognizable human experience, not to group related vocabulary.

Every design decision should strengthen the player's ability to recognize one coherent lived moment rather than identify semantic categories or lexical relationships.

**Principle 2 — Recognition Before Spectacle**

Build recognizable patterns of human life.

Do not rely on novelty, cultural references, fictional worlds, historical events, or spectacle as substitutes for recognition.

Moments should become memorable because they are deeply human—not because they are unusually dramatic.

Recognition should arise from plausible human experience that a player could reasonably experience, witness, or meaningfully imagine.

**Principle 3 — Trust the Player**

Provide meaningful evidence.

Do not over-explain.

Do not narrate emotions.

Do not tell the player what to think.

Allow the player's own understanding, memory, and imagination to complete the experience.

**Principle 4 — Every Clue Earns Its Place**

Every clue must contribute distinct, meaningful evidence toward reconstructing the hidden moment.

No clue exists merely to fill space, repeat another clue, or decorate the board.

Each clue should justify its presence by improving recognition.

**Principle 5 — Protect the Learning Experience**

The puzzle should remain welcoming and intellectually honest for language learners.

Difficulty should arise from meaningful inference rather than unnecessary vocabulary complexity, visual clutter, ambiguity, or arbitrary challenge.

The game does not shame the player for uncertainty.

Curiosity is supported.

Discovery is rewarded.

Revealed answers are part of learning—not evidence of failure.

### Constitutional Safeguards

The Constitutional Principles define what the game exists to protect.

The Constitutional Safeguards define how we determine whether those principles have actually been honored.

A principle is not considered fully honored until its corresponding safeguard has been satisfied.

Safeguards are not optional quality improvements.

They are constitutional tests.

Current safeguards include:

**Recognition Test**

Ask: Does this puzzle invite recognition through shared human experience, or does it rely on spectacle, cultural familiarity, or media recognition to become interesting?

Recognition should come from plausible patterns of human life—not from celebrity, fiction, historical events, memes, or shared media references.

**Ambiguity Audit**

A puzzle must lead toward one coherent hidden moment.

Clues that reasonably support multiple unrelated moments weaken recognition and reduce the distinct contribution of every clue.

If ambiguity prevents confident reconstruction of the intended experience, the puzzle requires revision before completion.

**Clue Contribution Test**

Every clue should answer the question: If this clue were removed, would the puzzle lose meaningful evidence?

If the answer is no, the clue has not earned its place.

**Final Author Check**

Before a puzzle is considered complete, ask:

- Does every clue contribute uniquely?
- Is the hidden moment recognizable?
- Is the challenge created by inference rather than confusion?
- Does the board invite glancing before close reading?
- Would this feel like a believable pattern of human life?
- Does the completed puzzle embody the five Constitutional Principles?

If any answer is no, continue authoring.

### Governance

The Design Constitution defines the enduring identity of The No-Name Game.

It should change rarely.

Future discoveries should normally be incorporated into:

- the Research Change Log,
- the Evidence Base,
- the Design Principles,
- the Authoring Framework,
- or the Canonical Prompt,

rather than by revising the Constitution itself.

The Constitution exists to preserve the project's deepest design commitments while allowing methods and implementation to evolve through evidence and continued experimentation.
