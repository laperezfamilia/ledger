# Beto Pérez — Discovery Session 3: Methodology Stress-Test (2026-08-05)

**Status: Working methodology only.** Continues numbering from Discovery Sessions 1–2 (observations 1–13, plus two Founder Decisions). Surfaced while organizing Checkpoint 1 of Beto's Discovery synthesis into the Discovery Memo and Reference Draft.

---

## 14. Adjacent Candidate Principles Collapsing into a Deeper Organizing Principle

**Observation:** more than once during Beto's discovery, several things that initially looked like separate candidate principles turned out, on reflection, to be behavioral expressions of one deeper principle rather than independent principles themselves. Curiosity, craft-appreciation, and question-asking collapsed into a single deeper attentional orientation. Separately, "seeks to understand the person before responding to the situation" (working name: Relational Orientation) was left explicitly open in the synthesis as possibly a downstream consequence of two other candidate principles combined, rather than a third independent one — see the Reference Draft for how that specific judgment call was made.

**Proposed methodological implication:** before treating a recurring behavior as its own Section I principle, actively test whether it might instead be a downstream expression of an already-identified deeper one. This reinforces, rather than newly discovers, Session 1's §5 ("Organizing Principles Must Be Earned," Behavior → Patterns → Organizing Principles) — this is that earlier suspicion showing up in practice, on a second character, which is itself worth noting as a small piece of replication evidence for it.

**Status:** working methodology, reinforcing an existing observation rather than introducing a new one.

## 15. Roots and Branches — A Hypothesis About Hierarchical Principle Structure

**Status: Experiment 2 hypothesis only, explicitly not to be treated as methodology yet — Jamie & Solara were clear about this distinction, and it's preserved here.**

**Observation:** the collapsing pattern in §14 suggests architectural principles might not sit flat, as Section I currently assumes — some principles ("roots") may generatively produce others ("branches") as natural downstream expressions, rather than every principle occupying the same structural level.

**Why this stays a hypothesis, not a proposal:** if it held, it would eventually imply Section I's flat numbered-list format needs to represent hierarchy, not just sequence — a real structural change. That's exactly the kind of preemptive structural modification the program already decided against in Session 2's Founder Decision on sequencing: identify and stress-test principles first, don't modify the structure ahead of the evidence. Recording the hypothesis now; not acting on it.

**Distinct from the Circumstances Filter (§13):** the Circumstances Filter tests whether a candidate statement is a principle *at all*. This hypothesis is about the relationship *between* confirmed principles, once identified — a different, later question.

## 16. Distinguishing Admiration from Personal Practice

**Observation, inferred from this session's framing (not given with a worked example — flagged as an inference rather than restated with false certainty):** evidence of Beto *admiring* a quality in someone else (the knife maker's years of craft, memoirists who lived alongside a culture rather than visiting it) and evidence of Beto *personally practicing* something himself (his morning run) appear to be doing different evidentiary work, even when they look similar on the surface.

**Proposed distinction:** admiration reveals what Beto *values* — useful as supporting evidence for attentional/orientation principles, but at arm's length from him. Personal practice reveals what Beto actually *does*, unprompted, repeatedly — stronger, more direct evidence, and the more likely source of Foundational Scenes specifically. Treating an admiration-scene and a practice-scene as equally strong evidence for the same principle risks quietly weakening the evidentiary discipline the Documentation Convention already asks for (Foundational vs. Supporting vs. Illustrative).

**Status:** working methodology, not yet tested against a case where it's ambiguous which category a scene belongs to.

## 17. Distinguishing Rhythms from Isolated Supporting Scenes

**Observation:** extends §11 (Rhythms of Living, Session 1). A single instance of a recurring rhythm — Beto sitting on the porch once, say — risks being filed as an ordinary Supporting Scene for some other principle, when what it's actually evidencing is the rhythm itself, which needs the recurring-instance treatment §11 already proposed (Recurring Structure → Varying Instances → Interpretation), not scene-by-scene treatment under a different principle entirely.

**Practical implication tested this session:** the Reference Draft (`./beto-character-architecture-reference-DRAFT-checkpoint-1.md`) actually uses the parallel Rhythm format proposed in §11 for the first time, rather than continuing to name it as a future friction point. Worth treating as the first real test of whether that proposed format works in practice, not just in principle.

**Status:** working methodology, first practical application pending review.
