# The Adopted Family Design Filter (Working Thesis)

**Status: Working Thesis, not canon — candidate architectural principle, treated as an open research agenda rather than adopted.** Part of the Design Research program (`./README.md`). Flagged by Jamie & Solara as potentially the single biggest operational discovery from the 2026-08-02 session; revised 2026-08-02 after a pressure-test review surfaced that the metaphor alone wasn't concrete enough on its own.

## Revision: a two-step process, not a single filter (2026-08-02)

The original single-question filter turned out to under-specify itself: asked "what would an adopted family do?" in the abstract, different writers could reasonably answer differently, because nothing pins down *which* family this is, tonally. Tellingly, the worked examples below never actually stayed abstract — each one immediately became "what would Rosa say?" or "what would Sofía say?" That's revealing rather than a flaw: **the family metaphor generates the values; Character Architecture operationalizes them.**

Revised as a two-step process:

**Step 1 — What would the Pérez family value in this moment?** This generates the direction (warmth, patience, honesty, not making someone feel small) — the family philosophy layer, not yet a decision.

**Step 2 — Which character naturally owns this moment, and what would they specifically do or say?** This is where Character Architecture takes over — the value gets expressed through one real, evidenced person's voice (Rosa's, Sofía's, Beto's), not a generic "family" register.

**The resulting layering model:**

> Family philosophy → Character Architecture → Product behavior

This replaces "family metaphor OR Character Architecture" with a pipeline where each layer depends on the one before it. Nothing here should be treated as bypassing Character Architecture's own evidence discipline (Foundational Scene, Supporting Scenes) — Step 2 still requires an actual reference for the character involved, not an improvised guess at their voice.

## Examples

Each example below is already the two-step process in miniature — Step 1 (family value) collapsed into Step 2 (a specific character's line), because that's how these were actually discovered.

**Notifications**

- Instead of: *"Your streak is ending."*
- Step 1: the family values presence over performance.
- Step 2 — Rosa: *"We've missed having breakfast with you."*

**Corrections**

- Instead of: *"Incorrect."*
- Think — Sofía: *"I know exactly what you meant. Can I show you how I'd probably say it?"*

**Returning after two weeks**

- Instead of: *"Welcome back!"*
- Think: *"Life gets busy. Come sit down and tell us what's been going on."*

## Related principle: products naturally exist inside the world

A second, closely related discovery from the same session: products should appear inside Casa Pérez because they genuinely belong in the characters' lives — not because they're being marketed.

**Natural:**
- Beto watches Sofía's newest YouTube video while waiting in an airport.
- Rosa prints one of Sofía's Etsy resources for work.
- Carmen proudly shares something Sofía made with a friend.

**Forced:**
- Characters interrupt a conversation simply to recommend an app.

**Pressure test:**

> If our products disappeared tomorrow, would this conversation still feel authentic?

If yes, it belongs.

*(`ledger` note: this pressure test and the two-step family/Character-Architecture filter are two angles on the same discovery — the family's world should feel real independent of the products in it, and product decisions should feel like something a specific family member would actually do. Kept in one file because they were discovered together and are meant to be applied together, not because they're literally the same claim. The disappearing-product pressure test is the more concrete of the two — binary and checkable — and is proposed as the acceptance test that candidate ideas generated by Step 1/Step 2 get run through, rather than the two being co-equal.)*

## Founder decision (pending): treat as a research agenda, not canon

Solara's recommendation, pending Jamie's confirmation: preserve the Adopted Family concept as a **candidate architectural principle**, explicitly held as an open research agenda rather than promoted. Questions to answer before promotion:

- Can it survive difficult business decisions (pricing, retention mechanics, data use) — not just tone and copy?
- Does it consistently guide multiple writers toward similar decisions, or does it stay loose enough to justify whatever was already planned?
- Does it measurably improve user trust?
- Where is the warmth/surveillance boundary — see `./recognition-vs-evaluation.md`'s "human-scale observation" note, which surfaced from the same review.
- Does it remain coherent across the entire ecosystem, not just the products it was first tested against?

**Status: not yet answered.** No promotion to canon or to a locked design principle should happen until at least the business-decision and multi-writer-consistency questions have real evidence behind them.
